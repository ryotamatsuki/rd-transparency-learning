## Minimal main-text citation set: 12–15 papers, organized by *economic channel* (not a chronological list)

### Group I: R&D investment observability and private/social incentives (3–4)

**Core:** \citep{BaikKim2020,Sengupta2016,JiangXiong2025}; \citep{BaikKim2025} in a short caveat if endogenous disclosure is mentioned.

Write: Prior work has shown that concealing an R&D decision can change firms' incentives and welfare; under differentiated Bertrand competition, observability can reduce investment while raising private profit and reducing welfare for a parameter subset. **Explicitly attribute that final configuration to Baik & Kim (2020, Section 7.3)**. Jiang & Xiong concern voluntary release of *endogenous realized cost information*, not a learning-capacity investment.

**Claim to avoid:** "We are the first to identify disclosure yielding higher duopoly profits but lower welfare."

### Group II: Experience-driven future efficiency and strategic pricing (3–4)

**Core:** \citep{Spence1981,CabralRiordan1994,BesankoDoraszelskiKryukov2014}; add \citep{BesankoDoraszelskiKryukov2019} for the welfare link.

Write: A firm may set a current price with a view to how the resulting sales change future cost or competitiveness. The present paper does **not** claim that market-share-driven learning or predatory price incentives are novel. The object introduced here is the **chosen learning coefficient** \(\lambda\), and how *disclosing its investment level* changes a competitor's current pricing incentives, conditional on endogenous s and b.

**Potential optional:** \citep{FudenbergTirole1983}. Avoid listing 5 basic papers in a single sentence when 2–3 establish the point.

### Group III: R&D direction, learning capacity, and disclosure-driven short horizon (4–5)

**Core:** \citep{CohenLevinthal1994,LinZhou2013,KimPoggi2026,HuangEtAl2023}; optional \citep{ChenLiangPetrov2024,CohenLevinthal1989}.

Write: Investment in learning capability and endogenous portfolios are familiar; information disclosure can steer firms toward quick-return innovation. Explain *why those mechanisms are not ours*: absorb external knowledge (Cohen & Levinthal), across-product portfolio specialization (Lin & Zhou), patent/innovation-race technology allocation (Kim & Poggi), capital market monitoring/managerial impatience (Huang/Chen). Our candidate mechanism is market-price defense interacting with own customer-experience learning.

**Caution:** Kim & Poggi (2026) is the **September 2026 revised working-paper version**. Never label its full text "read and compared" unless the revised text has actually been acquired and checked. Huang et al. is empirical plus conceptual analysis, not the same analytical duopoly game.

### Group IV: disclosure/innovation-race/Hotelling foundations (only if needed)

**Selective:** \citep{Gill2008,LeeWu2026,Vives1984,GalOr1985,Hotelling1929,dAspremontGabszewiczThisse1979}. ## Ready-to-expand English structure (notes, not finished prose)

**Paragraph A — Closest observability results (approximately 90–120 words):**
"Observability of R&D is known to alter investment incentives and private and social outcomes. [BaikKim2020, Sengupta2016] In a differentiated Bertrand extension, [BaikKim2020, Section 7.3] obtain a regime in which observed investment is lower and firm profit higher while welfare falls. A distinct line studies strategic disclosure of endogenous cost information [JiangXiong2025]. Our analysis therefore does not seek novelty in the sign of the disclosure-profit-welfare comparison. Instead, it asks whether the disclosed input is a learning capability whose payoff depends endogenously on current customer acquisition."

**Paragraph B — Market learning mechanism (~80–100 words):**
"The dynamic effects of current prices and sales through learning-by-doing are established in [Spence1981,CabralRiordan1994,BesankoDoraszelskiKryukov2014]. Our departure is that the entrant chooses the rate at which customer experience is transformed into future quality, and a rival may condition its first-period price on the publicly observed rate. The induced price response alters the entrant's choice between immediate quality improvement and future learning capability."

**Paragraph C — Direction of R&D (~100–130 words):**
"R&D can build an ability to learn [CohenLevinthal1994] and competition can redirect R&D portfolios [LinZhou2013]. Technology research versus development under strategic concealment is studied in [KimPoggi2026]. Furthermore, mandatory disclosure can create a short-horizon innovation bias through capital-market pressure [HuangEtAl2023], while financial reporting affects technological exploration choices [ChenLiangPetrov2024]. The present model isolates a different, purely product-market reason for such reorientation: observability permits a competitor to defend its current customer base, reducing the future return to experience-dependent R&D."

**Final contribution sentence:** say "We characterize sufficient conditions for..." not "We discover the first...". Mention strategic effects and conditional private/social rankings; no universal disclosure recommendation.
